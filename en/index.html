Christophe Thurnherr

The Art of Marquetry

Unique artworks in precious wood — the art of elevating raw material, from sketch to finished lacquered piece.

Biography

It was in Belfort, in eastern France, where he was born, that he chose at a young age to pursue a career as a cabinetmaker. Several years working in high-end interior fitting allowed him to build expertise in the techniques of working with different veneer wood species.

Step by step, in order to uncover all the secrets of this demanding discipline, he built his marquetry skills as a self-taught artist. Since 2012, he has put all his expertise into creating figurative works of art.

In his works, mass-dyed wood species predominate in a subtle blend of polychromy and natural wood. His quest for perfection is marked by technical virtuosity and a highly developed sense of detail.

An unconventional vision and approach to marquetry, breaking free from centuries-old conventions — the intent being to bring a fresh perspective to work that surprises and provokes thought.

The themes explored include motorsport, Riva boats, watches, coats of arms, as well as themed chess sets. Today, his expertise also allows him to take on any other commission.

Craftsmanship

Wood marquetry art involves composing a picture from a multitude of wood species cut with a fine-bladed saw. Sourced from all five continents, Christophe's palette comprises 45 meticulously selected precious wood species, as well as mother-of-pearl, bone, gold leaf, precious metals, and mass-dyed woods.

Plays of light and shadow create a 3D trompe-l'œil effect, while a multi-layer lacquer finish enhances the sense of depth. In his pursuit of perfection, his creations are marked by meticulous attention to detail.

His creations range from Automotive Art to Wildlife Art, from Coats of Arms to bespoke reproductions. Christophe Thurnherr adapts to every request.

Creating a piece requires a minimum of 250 individual parts — up to 2,700 for the most exclusive works — requiring several months of work.

Moonwatch — The Genesis of an Artwork

About the artwork

A marquetry artwork on the theme of an icon of Swiss watchmaking, the Omega Speedmaster Professional watch. In this artistic interpretation, it is depicted in lunar orbit amid the shimmer of the solar system's 8 planets.

This piece required close to 1,000 elements cut with a scroll saw from around fifteen precious wood species. All cuts are made using a head-mounted magnifier at 2.5x magnification.

Fossil Oak — 5,000 Years Old

This rare wood, extracted from the peat bogs of Brière in Loire-Atlantique, displays shades ranging from brown to raven-wing black. These were originally forests bordering great rivers, uprooted by climatic upheavals.

The trunks, submerged in peat and deprived of oxygen, undergo a long fossilization process over several thousand years. The wood becomes charged with minerals, making it as hard as stone.

Princely Coat of Arms of Monaco

Technique & Craftsmanship — Princely Coat of Arms of Monaco

I chose to base this work on the official vector artwork, respecting all the heraldic conventions governing the Princely Coat of Arms.

For aesthetic and artistic reasons, I took the liberty of adding detail to the figures and the rest of the design that make up the coat of arms.

This marquetry was created using the piece-by-piece technique. This highly complex method involves around twenty steps, cutting every piece individually according to a technical line drawing.

The pieces are then assembled one by one to bring the subject and final image to life, much like a painter creating a canvas. All cutting is done with a scroll saw fitted with a 0.2 mm blade.

This unique piece is made up of over 1,000 elements. The most complex part is the collar of the Order of Saint-Charles: the microscopic size of certain elements pushed me to the limits of the technique, cutting the 300 pieces of the medal with its small 20 mm-diameter crown from no fewer than 100 pieces.

At the top, the crown is made of Ceylon satinwood, and the pearls are in black and white mother-of-pearl. At the bottom, some pearls are made from Amboyna burl, a very rare wood species from Asia.

At the center, the diamond shapes on the shield are in red ash and bleached white ash. The red mantle is in dyed sycamore, the white ermine lining in bleached spotted maple. The figures' robes are in elm, their faces in alder. The 'Deo Juvante' ribbon is in aromatic cedar.

The background (recessed) is in Italian walnut. The frame is in elm in the colours of the Principality. The edges of the frame are finished with 24-carat gold leaf, sealed between two coats of lacquer.

Monaco Grand Prix 2024

Technique & Craftsmanship — Monaco Grand Prix 2024

The poplar background, selected for the quality of its grain, was hollowed out with a scalpel; all other elements were cut with a scroll saw. The precision and quality of the cutting make the joints imperceptible to the naked eye.

The tyres are cut from fossil oak, an exceptional wood prized for its rarity as well as its distinctive aesthetic qualities. This wood, extracted from the peat bogs of Brière in Loire-Atlantique, displays shades ranging from brown to raven-wing black.

These were originally forests bordering rivers, uprooted by climatic upheavals. Over time, the trunks — deprived of oxygen through immersion several metres deep in the peat — absorb minerals from the iron-rich soil and undergo a long fossilization process spanning several thousand years, with some carbon-14 datings ranging from 2,000 to 6,000 years. Only 20% of the extracted material is usable.

The Automobile Club logo alone comprises 270 elements and required considerable technical expertise, given that some of the pieces measure no more than 0.2 mm.

All the polychrome tints are achieved from specific white woods, immersed in a 70°C aqueous solution with colour pigments and a fixative; the wood veneers remain in the hot water for several days so that the wood is thoroughly impregnated to its core.

This complex piece of marquetry required around twenty steps to complete.
